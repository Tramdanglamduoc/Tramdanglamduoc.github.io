1

Vietnam: from the bench to the data

Until 2023 · and back for summer schools

I started in the wet lab. In high school I synthesized iron-oxide nanoparticles for cancer hyperthermia and MRI, and studied a behavioral therapy based on memory reconsolidation. Then I met the same scanner from the other side: at MaSSP Data Science 2024, my team trained brain-tumor MRI classifiers with federated learning, so hospitals could share a model without sharing patients' scans.

  • Federated learning for brain tumor classification, MaSSP 2024 (project)
  • Second Prize, ViSEF (Ho Chi Minh City round); Gold Award, IYRIC
  • VIASM summer schools in optimization, mathematical physics and applied mathematics (2026)

I first knew MRI from the materials side. MaSSP showed me its data side, and how often medical data is too private to share.

2

Latvia: making the most of limited data

BSc Computer Systems, Riga Technical University · 2023–2026

At RTU I learned how computers are built, from hardware to networks, and how research is done. My thesis asked how knowledge from large public skin-lesion datasets can help a model trained on a small multispectral dataset, using multistage transfer learning and image-plus-metadata fusion. As a research assistant, I co-authored a paper at ENASE 2026 and a systematic literature review.

  • Thesis on multispectral skin imaging, grade 9/10
  • Baltic University Programme 2024 (1 of 90 from 600 applicants)
  • Student ambassador, Study in Latvia

Transfer learning helps, but data alone has limits. What if the model also understood the physics of the body?

4

Türkiye: adding physics to AI

Summer research, Özyeğin University, Istanbul · 2026

In the Biomedical Physics track, I worked on a physics-informed framework for estimating forces and giving haptic feedback in simulated robotic surgery, and built TissueSense, a simulator based on viscoelastic tissue models. It was my step from diagnosis toward intervention.

Physics gives a model rules. Clinical decisions also need medical knowledge.

5

Taiwan: adding medical knowledge

TEEP research internship, Taipei Medical University · Oct 2026 –

I'm building tools that collect and connect biomedical literature, using knowledge graphs and retrieval-augmented generation, so researchers can find what matters faster.

Every step so far has asked the same question: how can medical AI stay reliable when data isn't enough?
